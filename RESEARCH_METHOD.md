# Research Method

Connected Records uses a source-based research method designed to keep different kinds of evidence separate and reproducible.

## 1. Define a bounded question

Begin with a specific transaction, organizational decision, historical claim, program, or institutional relationship.

Record the relevant dates, entities, and population or activity being examined.

## 2. Build the document base

Collect original or primary records whenever available, including:

- government records
- institutional investigations
- university reviews
- tax and foundation filings
- contemporaneous publications
- correspondence
- grant records
- conference materials
- program records
- financial disclosures

Preserve exact titles, archival identifiers, URLs, and retrieval dates.

## 3. Use artificial intelligence for extraction, then verify manually

Artificial intelligence may be used to draft tables of entities, dates, amounts, events, and source locations.

Every material finding is checked against the original record.

AI summaries, generated diagrams, and search snippets are not treated as source documents.

## 4. Resolve entity identity

Organizations, foundations, subsidiaries, programs, business groups, and individuals are treated as distinct entities unless the record establishes otherwise.

Full legal names are used where they can be confirmed.

## 5. Reconcile financial records carefully

For each financial record, preserve:

- payer
- recipient
- amount
- currency
- date or fiscal year
- transaction type
- program or institutional context

A proposed budget, maximum contribution, grant ceiling, or commitment is not treated as money paid unless a payment record supports that conclusion.

Institution-wide funding is not automatically assigned to a particular event or program.

## 6. Classify the evidence

Different record types are kept separate.

**Reported payment**  
A payment or receipt represented in a specified government, foundation, institutional, or financial record.

**Authorization or proposed amount**  
An approval, budget, commitment, ceiling, or proposed arrangement that may not equal a completed payment.

**Program or professional connection**  
A documented collaboration, introduction, meeting, report, institutional role, public role, or program relationship. These are not financial-transfer edges.

**Unresolved assertion**  
An allegation, participant statement, third-party description, or incomplete record that requires independent corroboration.

## 7. Prevent double counting

Related records may describe the same transaction.

Amounts are not added together without checking for:

- duplicate descriptions
- different reporting periods
- pledges versus payments
- original versus amended grants
- institution-wide versus event-specific funding
- repeated reporting of the same transaction across different records

## 8. Preserve uncertainty

Missing records are not converted into proof that an event did not occur.

Conflicting accounts are preserved as conflicting accounts.

Open questions remain explicitly unresolved until additional evidence is located.

Visual links are added only where the record supports the connection being shown.

## 9. Make the work reproducible

Each documented connection should be traceable to a stable source reference or research-section identifier.

Corrections should identify the affected connection, the reason for the correction, and the source supporting the revision.

## Suggested extraction instruction

For each original document, extract:

- source title
- source URL or archival identifier
- page or folio
- date
- full legal name of sender and recipient
- exact type of connection
- amount and currency, if stated
- whether the amount is proposed, approved, reported paid, or independently confirmed
- the narrowest interpretation supported by the record
- conflicting evidence
- the primary record needed to resolve any uncertainty

If a fact is not visible in the record, report:

**NOT ESTABLISHED**
